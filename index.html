CS-E4740: Federated Learning

Aalto University · Alexander Jung

Spring 2027. Lectures run 1 March – 14 April 2027. Aalto students enrol through Sisu. Everyone else can follow along for free — assignments, deadlines and announcements, no enrolment needed.

Catalog description

Federated learning trains machine learning models across many devices that never share their raw data. The course builds it from a single design principle: a federated system is a graph whose nodes hold local datasets, and the algorithms — FedGD, FedAvg, FedProx, clustered federated learning — follow from applying different optimization methods to generalized total variation minimization over that graph.

The course then takes the parts that actually stop practitioners: how many training rounds buy how much accuracy, how to find which devices should learn together when you cannot see their data, how many malicious participants a protocol survives, what differential privacy costs in utility, and when an exchanged model parameter is still personal data under the GDPR and the EU AI Act.

Objectives

On completion, students can

  • model federated learning applications using graphs;
  • use total variation minimization as a design principle for federated learning;
  • design federated learning algorithms by applying optimization methods to total variation minimization;
  • analyze the computational and statistical properties of federated learning algorithms.

Textbook

A. Jung, Federated Learning: From Theory to Practice. Singapore: Springer Nature Singapore, 2026 (doi:10.1007/978-981-95-1009-2), with the preprint free on arXiv:2505.19183. The course also draws on Machine Learning: The Basics (Springer, 2022) for the recap in the first lecture.

Prerequisites

Empirical risk minimization and the basics of supervised learning; gradient descent; linear algebra; Python with numpy and scikit-learn. Graph theory is developed from scratch, and no prior knowledge of distributed systems is assumed.

Intended audience

Master's and doctoral students in computer science, data science and electrical engineering, and practitioners who need to train models on data that cannot be centralized — health, industrial, mobile and public-sector data.
