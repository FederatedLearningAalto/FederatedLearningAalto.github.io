Federated Learning

CS-E4740 · Aalto University · Alexander Jung

Federated learning trains machine learning models across many devices that never share their raw data. This graduate course builds it from one design principle: a federated system is a graph whose nodes hold local datasets, and the algorithms — FedGD, FedAvg, FedProx, clustered federated learning — all follow from applying different optimization methods to generalized total variation minimization over that graph.

Everything is free and public: recordings, slides, assignments, code and the textbook. Aalto students take it for 5 ECTS; anyone else can follow along at no cost and without enrolling.

Material

  • Lecture recordings on YouTube
  • Textbook: Federated Learning — From Theory to Practice, free on arXiv
  • Slides, assignments and code on GitHub

Topics

  • From machine learning to federated learning
  • The design principle: empirical graphs and generalized total variation minimization
  • Federated optimization: FedGD, FedAvg, convergence
  • FedProx, FedRelax and personalization
  • Federated clustering and clustered federated learning
  • Legal frameworks: the GDPR and the EU AI Act
  • Robustness: Byzantine attacks, data and model poisoning
  • Privacy: differential privacy, privacy attacks, secure aggregation
